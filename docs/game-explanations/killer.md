Killer
Objective

The goal of the game is to be the last player remaining alive.

A game requires a minimum of 3 players.

Assigning Player Numbers

Before the actual game starts, each player is assigned a unique dartboard number.

Players take turns throwing one dart with their non-dominant hand (the hand they normally do not throw with).

The number they hit becomes their personal number for the rest of the game.

Duplicate numbers

If a player hits a number that has already been assigned to another player, they do not receive that number.

Instead, they may choose one of the two neighbouring numbers on the dartboard.

If one of those neighbouring numbers is already taken, continue moving in that same direction around the dartboard until you reach the first available number.

Example

Player 1 hits 20.

Player 2 also hits 20.

Player 2 may choose either:

1
5

If 1 is already taken, Player 2 may instead choose between:

5
18

because 18 is the next available number in that direction.

This process continues until a free number is found.

Bullseye During Number Selection

If a player hits either the Single Bull or the Double Bull while selecting their number, they do not receive a number immediately.

Instead:

Wait until every player has had their turn.
After all normal numbers have been assigned, every Bull player may freely choose any remaining unassigned number.

If multiple players hit Bull, the order in which they choose is determined randomly.

Missing the Board

Throwing with your non-dominant hand is difficult.

If a player completely misses the dartboard with their first throw, they receive a second attempt.

If they miss again, they receive a third and final attempt.

If, after three attempts, they still have not hit the dartboard:

Wait until every other player has had the opportunity to obtain a number.
Players who hit Bull first choose their numbers.
Finally, the entire group decides which remaining number will be assigned to each player who failed to hit the board after all three attempts.

At this point, every player should have a unique assigned number and the real game begins.

Starting the Game

Every player starts with:

1 Life
Rank: Recruit

Players always throw three darts per turn.

The turn order is determined by the assigned numbers.

The player with the lowest assigned number starts first.

The remaining players follow in ascending numerical order.

For example:

1 → 5 → 8 → 12 → 20

Player 20 always throws last if number 20 is assigned.

If a player dies during the game, their turn is simply skipped from then on.

Life Ranks
Lives	Rank
❤️ 1	Recruit
❤️ 2	Hunter
❤️ 3	Slayer
❤️ 4	Predator
❤️ 5	Killer
Becoming a Killer

Players begin with 1 Life.

Before becoming a Killer, a player may only gain lives for themselves by hitting their own assigned number.

Scoring on your own number gives:

Single = +1 Life
Double = +2 Lives
Triple = +3 Lives

A player must reach exactly 5 Lives.

Once a player reaches exactly 5 Lives, they become a Killer.

Bouncing Over 5 Lives

A player may never have more than 5 Lives.

If a scoring throw would increase a player's lives beyond 5, the excess "bounces back."

Example:

A player has 4 Lives.

They hit a Triple on their own number.

Normally they would gain 3 Lives and reach 7.

Since the maximum is 5:

7 is 2 above the limit.

Those 2 excess lives are subtracted from 5.

The player therefore ends up with 3 Lives.

What a Killer Can Do

Once a player becomes a Killer:

They can no longer gain additional lives.
They may now attack other players by aiming for their assigned numbers.

Hitting another player's assigned number removes lives from that player.

Damage dealt:

Single = -1 Life
Double = -2 Lives
Triple = -3 Lives

A Killer may attack any opponent at any time.

There is no defensive mechanic.

A player can only damage other players after becoming a Killer.

Exact Zero Rule

A player dies only when they reach exactly 0 Lives.

If damage would reduce a player below zero, the value "bounces back."

Examples:

1 → hit for 2 → becomes 1
1 → hit for 3 → becomes 2

This bounce rule only applies when a player would go below zero.

Death

A player who reaches exactly 0 Lives is dead and is immediately eliminated from the game.

Their assigned number is removed from play.

Future turns simply skip that player.

Hitting Yourself as a Killer

Once a player is a Killer, hitting their own assigned number no longer grants lives.

Instead, they lose lives.

Scoring on your own number while being a Killer:

Single = -1 Life
Double = -2 Lives
Triple = -3 Lives

Example:

A Killer has 5 Lives.

They hit a Triple on their own number.

They now have 2 Lives.

If they hit another Triple on themselves, they would normally go below zero.

Instead, the bounce rule applies and they return to 5 Lives.

A Killer can therefore never kill themselves.

Bull During the Game

Bull can also be used during the main game.

If a player hits the Single Bull, it acts as a Joker.

If the player is not yet a Killer, it grants +1 Life.
If the player is a Killer, they may remove 1 Life from any player of their choice.

If a player hits the Double Bull, it also acts as a Joker.

If the player is not yet a Killer, it grants +2 Lives.
If the player is a Killer, they may remove 2 Lives from any player of their choice.
Turns

Every player must always throw all three darts during their turn.

Even if:

they eliminate another player,
they become a Killer,
or anything else happens,

their turn continues until all three darts have been thrown.

Winning the Game

The game immediately ends when only one player remains alive.

That player is declared the winner.
